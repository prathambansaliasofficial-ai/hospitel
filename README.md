# Hospitel

**A proposed unified digital healthcare access and medical infrastructure platform for India.**

> **Status: concept / proposal.** This repository contains documentation only; no application code has been built. The original long-form concept document is preserved in [`docs/CONCEPT.md`](docs/CONCEPT.md).

The name combines *Hospital* and *Telephone*: bringing essential hospital-related services closer to people through a mobile device. Hospitel is envisioned as a government-led, public-welfare digital infrastructure connecting patients, hospitals, doctors, diagnostic centres, pharmacies, medicine suppliers and logistics providers through interoperable systems. It is not intended as a private healthcare marketplace.

## Problem

- **Fragmented records:** prescriptions, reports, bills and discharge summaries are scattered across hospitals and paper files, causing repeated tests and poor continuity of care, especially for elderly and chronic patients.
- **Difficult access:** discovering verified doctors and booking appointments often requires contacting hospitals individually.
- **Medication adherence:** patients on multiple medicines may miss or confuse doses; a basic alarm does not record whether doses were taken.
- **Medicine affordability:** patients often do not know whether a lower-cost generic equivalent of a prescribed molecule exists.

## Proposed Modules

1. **Digital medical records and patient history:** documents generated by participating providers (prescriptions, test and imaging reports, discharge summaries, vaccination records) linked to a digital health identity and accessible across hospitals with patient consent.
2. **Doctor and hospital appointments:** discovery of verified providers and digital booking.
3. **Medicine reminders and adherence:** reminders plus a record of reported or confirmed doses, with caregiver visibility.
4. **Affordable medicines and delivery:** identification of the prescribed molecule, generic alternatives, price comparison, quality and trust considerations, and integration with existing government programmes.

A related section proposes a partner-based distribution model, including India Post and private logistics providers as potential partners.

## Design Principles

Patient-centric care · continuity of care · interoperability · affordability · transparency · privacy and security (consent, access control, auditability) · building on existing public digital infrastructure rather than duplicating it.

## Architecture (Proposed)

Application layer, backend services, data and interoperability layer, and a security and access-control layer. The concept document details each layer.
